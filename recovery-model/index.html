How to read it

Each branch is a capacitor. A session pulls charge out of it, and it recharges through its own time constant τ. The pie shows which branch holds most of the missing charge right now, so it tells you what is actually limiting you today. Phosphocreatine refills in minutes, so it never shows up at day scale.

Notes. Phosphocreatine also stands in for metabolite clearance and nerve drive, which recover on a similar clock. Liver glycogen refills in about 12 hours with ordinary meals and only matters in long efforts or fasted training. Biofitness also covers red blood cell mass, tendon and bone, which sit closer to three or four months. Muscle glycogen refills more slowly while fibre damage is high, so those two branches are coupled.

🧮 Equations

Per hour, for each branch b with fatigue state x_b: x_b ← x_b · exp(−m_b / τ_b) + load_b(session) + leak_b(stress, alcohol, illness) m_b = sleep boost while asleep, lower while awake, chosen so 7.5 h of sleep averages to 1 readiness_b = 100 · S_b / (S_b + x_b) (S_b is the load that halves readiness) Loads per session: TRIMP = minutes · HRr · 0.64 · e^(1.92·HRr), HRr = (HRavg − HRrest) / (HRmax − HRrest) or minutes · RPE · 0.3 when there is no heart rate vagal = TRIMP · (1 + heat), heat = 0.15 · clamp((T + Td − 100) / 90, 0, 1) glyco = TRIMP · 0.8 fibre = 2 · km · (1 + gain_m / 200) for runs, minutes · RPE / 10 · 1.5 · (2 if eccentric) for strength Biofitness = 42-day exponential average of daily TRIMP Closed loop (extended Kalman filter on x_vagal): ln HRV ≈ μ − 0.40 · d d = x_v / (S_v + x_v) HR_end ≈ H0 + 12 · d HR drop in 60 s ≈ R0 − 10 · d RHR ≈ RHR0 + 6 · d each measurement nudges x_v by K · residual, and the baselines μ, H0, R0, RHR0 drift slowly

8 · Known limits
#⚠️ LimitWhat to do about it
1The loads are linear. Real overreaching is not: past a point, extra load produces a delayed collapse that a sum of exponentials cannot show.Treat a week of falling HRV despite rest as a red flag the model will understate.
2HRV mostly sees the vagal branch. It barely registers glycogen or fibre damage.The step test adds a second sensor; soreness and grip strength are worth a note in the log.
3Apple Watch reports HRV as SDNN, most apps report rMSSD. They are different numbers.Pick one source and never mix them. The baseline is learned, so the absolute level does not matter.
4Gains like 0.40, 12 bpm and the branch scales are starting points from the literature, not fitted to you.After a month of calibrations, the residuals show which way they are off.
5Nutrition and hydration are invisible to the model, and they move HRV just like fatigue does.Log big days of under-eating as stress, and note dehydration before a calibration.
