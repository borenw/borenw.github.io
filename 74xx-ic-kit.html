74HCxx / 74LSxx IC Kit

Pinout and internal function diagram for all 22 device types in the Minidodoca 22-value / 44-piece kit — 2 pieces of each part, plus DIP-14, DIP-16 and DIP-20 sockets. One page per part: the package pinout, the logic that is actually inside the die, and the truth table.

The two families in this kit

74LS — low-power Schottky TTL

  • VCC 4.75 – 5.25 V only (5 V, ±5 %).
  • Input LOW ≤ 0.8 V, input HIGH ≥ 2.0 V.
  • A floating input reads as HIGH and picks up noise — tie unused inputs to VCC through 1 kΩ, or to GND.
  • Output sinks ~8 mA but sources only ~0.4 mA — wire LEDs from VCC into the pin, not from the pin to ground.
  • Draws several mA even when nothing is switching.

74HC — high-speed CMOS

  • VCC 2 – 6 V. Runs at 3.3 V or 5 V.
  • Switching threshold sits near VCC/2; inputs draw essentially no current.
  • Inputs must still never be left open — an open CMOS input oscillates and heats the chip.
  • Outputs source and sink ±4 mA (±6 mA on the '245 and '595), swinging rail to rail.
  • Near-zero idle current, but a 74HC input is not guaranteed to see a 74LS HIGH of 2.4 V — use a 74HCT part when an LS output has to drive a CMOS input.

How to read these pages

Pinout drawings

  • Drawn top view: notch up, pin 1 at the top left, numbering counter-clockwise. That is exactly how the part looks sitting in a breadboard.
  • Power pins are not always the corners — the 74LS90 puts VCC on pin 5 and GND on pin 10.
  • NC means no connection: leave it open, do not use it as a tie point.

Function diagrams

  • Numbers in circles are package pin numbers.
  • A bubble on a gate input or output, or a bar over a name (CLR), means that signal is active LOW.
  • A small triangle at a flip-flop input marks the clock; C1 / 1D is the standard IEC notation for "this D input is controlled by clock 1".
Before you power anything up: put a 0.1 µF ceramic capacitor between VCC and GND right at each chip, check the notch orientation, and never let two outputs drive the same node. Series-resistor every LED (220 – 470 Ω at 5 V).

74HCxx / 74LSxx IC Kit — pinout & function reference74LS24524 / 25
74HC164
App note — wired as a single D flip-flop (D, CLK, Q)
74HC · high-speed CMOS
This kit has no 74HC74, so the only dedicated D flip-flop is the bipolar 74LS74 on page 15. When you want a CMOS flip-flop — and want to keep the '74's preset and clear free for set/reset work later — take the first stage of a 74HC164. Each of its eight stages already is a positive-edge D flip-flop; you simply use stage A and ignore the rest — its full internal view is on page 21.

Numbers in circles are 74HC164 package pins.

Positive-edge triggered, exactly like a '74 — but with no /Q output.

Worth knowing

No /Q
The '164 brings out only true outputs. If you need the complement, invert QA through a spare gate on the 74HC04 or 74HC00.
Free async reset
/CLR on pin 9 is already an active-LOW asynchronous clear. Tie it HIGH to ignore it, or drive it and you have D, CLK, Q and reset.
Free delay taps
QB…QH are the same data 2…8 clocks later — useful as an edge synchroniser or a shift-register debounce without adding a part.
Why not the others
The 74HC595's first stage is not brought out — its serial output is 8 clocks late — and the 74HC165 loads the wrong direction.
